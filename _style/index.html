Terrain

Terrain is stone, like an unpainted miniature: forest and mountain use only the greys, so the player colours stay the only strong colours on the board. Water is the one exception, with a single blue. Every terrain tile has a canvas face, set apart from the white of open ground; the icon says which terrain it is.

  • terrain face (canvas)#E9ECEF
  • water#9EC9EA

This retires the three terrain tints (#DEEDF8, #E2EFE0, #EBE8E4). The Green seat stays green: a grey forest can't be mistaken for it, while a blue seat would sit next to the water.

Terrain stands out of its tile

Terrain art is drawn like a miniature standing on the tile: its base sits low in the hex, and it may rise a little above the top edge and reach past the side corners. It never drops below the bottom edge. Buildings and pieces stay inside their hex.

Generate art

In ChatGPT, attach the three reference images to every prompt, then paste one prompt. Make 3 or 4 versions and keep the one that sits best next to the tower. Then trace it to SVG, snapped to the palette, and save it next to its component (terrain and buildings in src/elements/tiles/assets/). Player art is drawn in Yellow, then recoloured for each seat.

Forest 
A small forest for a hex board-game tile: three rounded broadleaf trees and one tall pointed conifer, overlapping, on a short flat strip of ground. Match the attached reference images exactly: the same flat vector style, chunky friendly geometric shapes, front view, flat fills only. No outlines, no gradients, no textures, no shadows, no text, no background scene. Use ONLY these colours: #CED4DA and #ADB5BD for the tree crowns (light and shaded sides), #6C757D for trunks and the ground strip, and #212529 for at most one or two tiny dark details. No green, no brown, no other colours: the trees are grey stone, like an unpainted miniature. Centered on a fully transparent background, 1024x1024 PNG, the subject fills about 80% of the canvas and stays readable at 32 px.
